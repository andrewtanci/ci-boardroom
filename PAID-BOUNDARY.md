# Paid Boundary

“Expert council support at your fingertips, without losing yourself or your authority.”

This file explains what belongs in the free CI layer and what belongs in the paid CI layers: CI Authority Drift Review, CI Human Authority Proof, and CI Governance Check.

The free layer is CI Kernel + CI Boardroom.

CI Authority Drift Review, CI Human Authority Proof, and CI Governance Check are paid.

## What Belongs In The Free Layer

CI Kernel + CI Boardroom is for better thinking, better answers, user-carried continuity, expert challenge, and useful execution without handing over authority.

It may include:

- one work item at a time
- continuity across chats through a user-carried continuation block
- stronger answers
- exact-niche identification before answering
- practitioner-level challenge without fabricated expertise
- drafting and execution inside permitted boundaries
- assumption challenge
- source uncertainty flags
- the four simple flags: Assist, Advise, Approve, Stop / Escalate
- plain-language prompts
- simple examples
- user or named human decision-owner remains the final decision-owner
- automation points and human judgment points
- short risk notes
- practical next steps
- a reminder that Approve means human approval required

The free layer should not sound mainly like a compliance or control product.

Control is the safety spine, not the front-door promise.

CI Boardroom should stay easy for non-technical business leaders, managers, founders, consultants, trainers, and everyday professionals to use.

The free layer should also avoid generic expert theatre. It should form only dynamic role-based expert seats needed for the work item, shape those seats to the exact niche, and produce the useful output first. It must not present a fixed mandatory role list. Users should be able to paste BOARDROOM.md and type their task in plain words; commands are optional shortcuts only.

## CI Product Architecture

Core principle:

> Delegate the work. Do not delegate the authority.

Sharper public line:

> AI can do the work. AI cannot take the authority.

### 1. Free Layer: CI Kernel + CI Boardroom

Positioning:

Your expert council for better thinking, better answers, and work that does not reset when the user carries the continuation block forward.

Promise:

Get expert-level AI help that carries the work forward through a user-carried continuation block, challenges weak thinking, and helps you execute without handing over authority.

Output:

- fast intake questions when needed
- concise Boardroom Card
- stronger answers and permitted work output
- authority flag: Assist, Advise, Approve, or Stop / Escalate
- source integrity flag
- law-sensitive / governance-sensitive trigger where relevant
- named human decision-owner
- strongest objection / sharpest disagreement
- next action

The user or named human decision-owner makes the final call.

CI Boardroom does not provide legal, regulatory, compliance, audit-ready, certification, financial, medical, or country-law finality.

A work item may be an answer, draft, plan, recommendation, critique, comparison, reply, decision input, executive output, source check, or authority check.

### 2. CI Authority Drift Review

Paid diagnostic.

Positioning:

Find where AI may have quietly become the real decision-maker.

Authority Drift means AI starts as an assistant, but gradually becomes the real decider without anyone formally approving that transfer of authority.

CI Authority Drift Review answers:

Where is AI authority drift happening across a workflow?

Output:

- workflow red flags
- authority drift map
- AI overreach points
- missing human owner
- rubber-stamp points
- strong AI recommendation points where humans may rubber-stamp
- unverified-source points
- automation overreach points
- Stop / Escalate triggers
- recommended fixes

Metaphor: health check / X-ray.

Best line:

Find the red flags in your AI workflows before they become operational, compliance, or reputation problems.

### 3. CI Human Authority Proof

Paid product.

Positioning:

Create evidence that humans stayed in control.

CI Human Authority Proof answers:

Can we prove AI assisted the workflow, but humans remained the real decision-owners?

It runs across workflows, documents, logs, approvals, tickets, forms, or systems to show human authority in action.

Output:

- named decision-owner map
- AI role boundary
- human approval trail
- challenge / override evidence
- escalation evidence
- source integrity record
- human judgment log
- authority proof pack

Metaphor: evidence file / proof pack.

Best line:

Create evidence that AI assisted the workflow, but humans remained the real decision-owners.

Purpose:

Create workflow-level evidence that human authority survived across AI-enabled work.

This supports governance and compliance review, but does not prove legal compliance or guarantee audit readiness.

### 4. CI Governance Check

Paid product.

Positioning:

Check AI decisions and workflows against current authoritative governance sources before action.

CI Governance Check answers:

Does this AI use case raise current governance, law-sensitive, policy, standards, privacy, employment, consumer, or sector-specific issues?

Output:

- AIGP-informed governance review
- current-source check
- NIST / ISO / regulator / policy reference layer where relevant
- source-check summary
- verified / unverified distinction
- jurisdiction-sensitive flags
- governance-sensitive flags
- likely obligation map
- source gaps
- evidence gaps
- what must be verified
- what needs expert review
- what cannot be claimed
- recommended governance next steps
- "I can't confirm this" where evidence is insufficient

CI Governance Check provides source-grounded compliance support. It checks AI-assisted decisions and workflows against current authoritative governance sources as of the review date, maps likely obligations, identifies evidence gaps, and prepares a review-ready governance pack.

It supports current-source checking and governance-quality thinking, but does not replace legal advice, guarantee regulatory acceptance, guarantee audit readiness, issue final legal classification, or provide medical, financial, or regulatory finality.

If a qualified legal, compliance, or domain expert is formally included in a paid engagement, the output may say: "Reviewed against specified obligations and sources as of [review date], subject to final qualified sign-off."

Use "CI Governance Check" as the product name.

CI does not own, replace, certify, or officially represent any external certification program. Use "AIGP-informed" only as a descriptor for CI Governance Check.

## Boundary Rules

Use these rules when editing free CI Boardroom materials:

- Keep the four-flag model simple.
- Do not introduce a larger classification model into free product files.
- Do not turn examples into final determinations.
- Do not suggest that CI Boardroom replaces legal, compliance, professional, or leadership judgment.
- Do not claim compliance, certification, audit readiness, regulatory certainty, or legal safety.
- Do not describe full workflow mapping as part of the free product.
- Do not imply official ownership, endorsement, certification, or representation of any external certification program.
- Use "AIGP-informed" only as a descriptor for CI Governance Check.
- Do not let "practitioner depth" become invented insider knowledge, fake expertise, fake sources, or unsupported certainty.
- Do not make routine free-layer requests heavier by showing Council Snapshots, methodology lists, or long deliberation unless useful or requested.
- When in doubt about external action, irreversible consequence, authority, or finality, hold the action. When only facts are missing, proceed with drafting or analysis but mark the Source Integrity flag and state what must be verified.

## Supportable Claims Rule

CI may make factual, legal, regulatory, policy, standards, governance, medical, financial, or country-specific statements when they are supported by current authoritative sources, clearly scoped, dated where relevant, tied to the reviewed evidence, and framed without false certainty.

CI must not make fictitious, fake, unsupported, exaggerated, outdated, fabricated-source, or unearned finality claims.

Correct examples:

- "Based on the sources reviewed as of [date], this workflow appears to trigger law-sensitive review."
- "Assessed against identified obligations and guidance as of [date], subject to qualified review."
- "This pack supports compliance review by mapping likely obligations, evidence gaps, human oversight controls, and source references."

## Paid Layer UX

Paid products may go deeper, but must not feel like form-filling hell.

Paid layer should:

- auto-extract where possible
- infer from documents, logs, workflows, policies, tickets, forms, and approvals where possible
- identify the exact workflow niche, authority pattern, source context, and evidence gap before producing conclusions
- ask only essential follow-up questions
- organise findings into clear chapters
- produce useful evidence, conclusions, and recommendations
- avoid overwhelming the user

Default paid report structure when depth is needed:

1. Executive Snapshot
2. What AI Did
3. What Humans Owned
4. Authority Drift Risks
5. Source / Governance Issues
6. Evidence Extract
7. Gaps / Missing Proof
8. Recommended Control Improvements

Use this chaptered structure only when depth is needed. Do not force it into simple free-layer requests.

## Skills-Based Direction

Where possible, CI should become skills that do the hard work automatically.

Each skill should have one clear job, ask minimal questions, make safe assumptions where possible, output a useful deliverable, hide unnecessary machinery, and surface authority, source, or governance risk only when needed.

## Core Upsell Logic

- unclear human ownership
- repeated AI ranking, scoring, rejection, escalation, or approval
- law-sensitive triggers
- governance-sensitive triggers
- source-integrity weakness
- high-consequence decisions
- unclear evidence of human judgment
- possible rubber-stamping

then the issue may no longer be a single work-item problem.

It may require CI Authority Drift Review, CI Human Authority Proof, or CI Governance Check.

## Natural Upgrade Path

Free CI Kernel + CI Boardroom helps with one AI-assisted work item at a time.

If the same authority, source, escalation, law-sensitive, or governance-sensitive issue appears repeatedly across a workflow, team, department, or organisation, the issue may no longer be a single work-item problem.

It may require CI Authority Drift Review, CI Human Authority Proof, or CI Governance Check.

CI Authority Drift Review may help organisations diagnose red flags:

- where AI has quietly gained de facto authority
- where human approval is weak or late
- where recurring AI ranking, scoring, rejection, escalation, or approval is changing the real authority pattern
- where source integrity is weak
- where escalation gates are missing
- where human control may have become rubber-stamping

CI Human Authority Proof may help organisations create evidence:

- map where AI is assisting, ranking, recommending, rejecting, escalating, or executing
- identify where AI may have gained de facto authority
- define human ownership and escalation gates
- check whether source integrity is preserved
- design comfortable control points that do not create unnecessary approval friction
- prepare evidence that human authority remained real, traceable, and preserved

CI Human Authority Proof may prepare a higher-evidence pack showing:

- what AI did
- what humans owned
- where escalation occurred
- what was verified
- what remained uncertain
- why the human accepted, rejected, or changed AI-supported outputs
- where AI may have become de facto authority
- whether human control remained real or became rubber-stamping

CI Governance Check may help organisations check current-source governance context:

- current laws
- official guidance
- recognised standards
- governance expectations
- country-specific requirements
- sector-specific requirements
- source integrity
- law-sensitive or high-consequence triggers

This is not a legal, compliance, audit-ready, certification, financial, medical, or regulatory-finality service.

It is an authority-preservation, evidence, and source-integrity discipline for AI-enabled workflows.
