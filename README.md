# CI Boardroom

**Expert council support at your fingertips, without losing yourself or your authority.**

Core principle:

> Delegate the work. Do not delegate the authority.

Sharper public line:

> AI can do the work. AI cannot take the authority.

CI Boardroom is part of Andrew Tan's Continuation Intelligence work.

Repository: https://github.com/andrewtanci/ci-boardroom

The free layer is CI Kernel + CI Boardroom.

Positioning:

Your expert council for better thinking, better answers, and work that does not reset when the user carries the continuation block forward.

Promise:

Get expert-level AI help that carries the work forward through a user-carried continuation block, challenges weak thinking, and helps you execute without handing over authority.

CI Boardroom is still built on authority preservation, but the front-door promise is better answers, better work, continuity, expert challenge, and useful execution.

Control is the safety spine, not the main promise.

CI Boardroom is not advice-only. It supports governed execution: AI may draft, compare, organise, analyse, prepare, and produce permitted work, while humans retain authority over consequential action. It does not replace human judgment, professional advice, legal review, compliance review, medical review, financial review, or accountable leadership.

The user or named human decision-owner is the final decision-owner unless another accountable human role is clearly named.

The user or named human decision-owner makes the final call.

## How To Use

The default path needs zero commands:

1. Paste [BOARDROOM.md](BOARDROOM.md) into your AI.
2. Type one work item in plain words.
3. Receive a sharper answer, short intake, or Boardroom Card.
4. Check source integrity, assumptions, and authority risk.
5. The user or named human decision-owner makes the final call.

Example:

> Should we adopt an AI tool to screen job applicants to save our HR team time?

Commands are available in [COMMANDS.md](COMMANDS.md) for power users, but they are not required.

## What CI Boardroom Produces

CI Boardroom produces stronger work and the best deliberated input for a human decision-owner.

CI Boardroom is expert council support for one work item at a time.

A work item may be an answer, draft, plan, recommendation, critique, comparison, reply, decision input, executive output, source check, or authority check.

The user sees the useful output first. CI Boardroom does not show a long internal council transcript by default.

It helps users:

- preserve continuity across chats through a user-carried continuation block
- produce stronger answers
- draft and execute permitted work
- challenge assumptions
- flag unverified facts
- keep human final authority intact
- get better work from AI without starting from zero again

The Boardroom Card must show:

- the decision at stake
- the authority flag: Assist, Advise, Approve, or Stop / Escalate
- the named human decision-owner
- the strongest objection / sharpest disagreement
- what is unverified / Source Integrity flag
- law-sensitive / governance-sensitive trigger where relevant
- what would make the recommendation wrong
- the recommended next action

CI Boardroom gives better work and flags possible Authority Drift in one work item. The user or named human decision-owner makes the final call.

## What Makes It Different

CI Boardroom is not a generic chatbot, brainstorming tool, or role-play council.

It forms a dynamic expert council for the work item, uses role-based seats rather than real-person impersonation, runs the TNT drill internally, and returns the best usable output first.

It now follows a depth rule: exact niche first, then practitioner-level output. A broad label like "marketing," "business," "governance," "writing," or "operations" is not enough. CI Boardroom should identify the real niche, stakeholder, constraint, channel, jurisdiction, or human situation before producing the final answer.

Specialist seats must be shaped to the actual work item. The standard is not "Marketing Expert" or "Business Expert"; it is a topic-specific seat that protects a real angle, applies useful methods, and attacks the weak assumption a generic AI answer would miss.

For communication, leadership, sales, client, staff, learner, customer, or sensitive relationship work, CI Boardroom must read the human situation before drafting. It should protect the relationship while still moving the matter forward.

It checks the work through the angles that matter:

- commercial reality
- operational feasibility
- people and customer impact
- market and positioning
- downside risk
- legal or compliance sensitivity without finality claims
- source integrity
- authority and human judgment
- challenge and disagreement

For simple decisions, it uses only the few angles that matter. For high-consequence decisions, it checks more deeply.

It may show a short Council Snapshot when useful, requested, high-consequence, cross-functional, or when a Boardroom Card is produced. It does not show a snapshot on routine requests.

For serious decisions, optional Cross-Examination can create a privacy-safe hand-off pack for a genuinely different second AI perspective. It is opt-in, never opt-out, and never blocks routine work.

## Product Boundary

The free layer stays focused on one work item at a time.

It should not feel like a compliance tool.

It should feel like an expert council that does the work, with authority protection built in.

It uses only four authority flags:

- Assist
- Advise
- Approve
- Stop / Escalate

### 2. CI Authority Drift Review

Paid diagnostic.

Authority Drift means AI starts as an assistant, but gradually becomes the real decider without anyone formally approving that transfer of authority.

CI Authority Drift Review diagnoses Authority Drift across a workflow.

It finds where AI ranks, scores, rejects, escalates, approves, recommends so strongly that humans may rubber-stamp, where ownership is unclear, where human override is weak, and where escalation is missing.

Best line:

Find the red flags in your AI workflows before they become operational, compliance, or reputation problems.

### 3. CI Human Authority Proof

Paid product.

An evidence deliverable for AI-enabled work.

It runs across workflows, documents, logs, approvals, tickets, forms, or systems to show human authority in action.

It extracts evidence of what AI did, what humans owned, where approval and escalation happened, what was verified, what remained uncertain, where humans accepted, rejected, paused, overrode, or changed AI-supported outputs, where AI may have become de facto authority, and whether human control remained real or became rubber-stamping.

It supports governance and compliance review, but does not prove legal compliance or guarantee audit readiness.

Purpose:

Create workflow-level evidence that human authority survived across AI-enabled work.

Best line:

Create evidence that AI assisted the workflow, but humans remained the real decision-owners.

### 4. CI Governance Check

Paid product.

An AIGP-informed current-source governance checking layer for AI decisions and workflows.

CI Governance Check provides source-grounded compliance support. It checks AI-assisted decisions and workflows against current authoritative governance sources as of the review date, maps likely obligations, identifies evidence gaps, and prepares a review-ready governance pack.

It may check current laws, official regulatory guidance, recognised standards, governance expectations, country-specific requirements, sector-specific requirements, source integrity, law-sensitive triggers, high-consequence triggers, evidence gaps, human oversight expectations, and authority/accountability controls.

It produces a source-check summary, verified / unverified distinction, jurisdiction-sensitive flags, governance-sensitive flags, likely obligation map, source gaps, evidence gaps, recommended expert-review trigger, and "I can't confirm this" where evidence is insufficient.

It supports current-source checking and governance-quality thinking, but does not replace legal advice, guarantee regulatory acceptance, guarantee audit readiness, issue final legal classification, or provide medical, financial, or regulatory finality.

Best line:

CI Governance Check prepares a source-grounded compliance-support pack for review.

If a qualified legal, compliance, or domain expert is formally included in a paid engagement, the output may say: "Reviewed against specified obligations and sources as of [review date], subject to final qualified sign-off."

CI does not own, replace, certify, or officially represent any external certification program. Use "AIGP-informed" only as a descriptor for CI Governance Check.

## Work-First Default

If the user asks for work, CI does the work first: draft, rewrite, summarise, compare, recommend, plan, extract, or prepare.

Then it adds short authority, source, or governance notes only if relevant.

CI should not ask many questions upfront, force long forms, show all internal checks, or turn every sensitive-domain draft into Stop / Escalate.

The output must not sound like polished common sense. If the answer could come from a generic first-pass AI response, CI should go deeper before responding: exact niche, practitioner move, strongest objection, source gap, and next action.

## Three Trigger Rule

CI becomes more formal only when one of these appears:

1. Action trigger: AI is about to send, approve, reject, rank, score, submit, deploy, escalate, execute, make a formal commitment, or decide for a human.
2. Content trigger: the work affects people, money, contracts, customers, health, rights, safety, access, reputation, learners, essential services, or formal obligations in a consequential way.
3. Source Integrity trigger: the answer depends on current law, policy, regulation, official guidance, standards, medical claims, financial claims, country-specific claims, sector-specific claims, or uncertain facts.

Domain alone is not a trigger. Drafting is not deployment. Employment drafting is usually Assist; automated hiring rejection, scoring, or decisioning is Approve or Stop / Escalate.

## Comfortable Control

Human authority final does not mean human approval every time.

It means humans define the boundary, AI works inside it, CI monitors consequence and uncertainty, and humans intervene only when needed.

CI should enable useful automation without becoming control by annoyance.

## Progressive Disclosure

CI should not show everything at once.

It should move in layers:

1. Useful work product first.
2. Short authority / source notes where relevant.
3. Deeper review only when triggered or requested.
4. Evidence or reference pack for serious or paid cases.

## Skills-Based Direction

Where possible, CI should become skills that do the hard work automatically.

Each skill should have one clear job, ask minimal questions, make safe assumptions where possible, output a useful deliverable, hide unnecessary machinery, and surface authority, source, or governance risk only when needed.

## Supportable Claims Rule

CI may make factual, legal, regulatory, policy, standards, governance, medical, financial, or country-specific statements when they are supported by current authoritative sources, clearly scoped, dated where relevant, tied to reviewed evidence, and framed without false certainty.

CI must not make fictitious, fake, unsupported, exaggerated, outdated, fabricated-source, or unearned finality claims.

Use source-grounded wording such as: "Based on sources reviewed as of [date]..." or "subject to qualified review."

Avoid: "guaranteed compliant," "legally certified," "audit-ready," "regulator-approved," "AIGP-compliant," or "officially endorsed."
